# Type 1 application catalog (Beaverton, administrative / no hearing)

This skill covers **Type 1** decisions only — administrative, no public hearing (procedure in BDC 50.35).
The four most common are **Sign Permit, DRCL, SDMHDR1, and LLD**. For any application, the authoritative
text is in the bundled chapters; always open the cited section and read it rather than relying on memory,
because thresholds and zone lists change by ordinance.

Two rules that apply to every intake:
- **Assumes fees are collected before review.** Don't list fee payment (50.25.1.G) as a
  missing completeness item.
- A complete application (50.25.1) always needs, at minimum: (A) the signed application form with owner
  consent or authorized-agent authorization, and (B) a written statement addressing the relevant approval
  criteria and development standards. Then add the **type-specific plans/exhibits** below.

## Sign Permit — BDC 40.60 (Type 1)

**Approval criteria (40.60.15.1.C):** satisfies threshold; submittal complete per 50.25.1; sign conforms to
**all of 60.40** (Sign Regulations); conforms to Engineering Design Manual vision-clearance standards;
related approvals sequenced.

**Determine the zone first — sign standards differ by zone:**
- Commercial / Industrial / Multiple-Use zones → **60.40.35** (wall, awning, projecting, blade, freestanding;
  Tables 60.40.35.A & .B for freestanding number/size/height).
- Residential zones (MR, RMA, RMB, RMC, CM-MR, CM-RM) → **60.40.40**.
- Downtown (RC-BC, RC-MU, RC-DT, RC-OT and the Downtown Design District) → base zone standards in 60.40.35
  **plus** the downtown design/materials standards in 60.40.35.6 and the Chapter 70 context; 40% window-sign
  allowance under 60.40.15.1.
- Always also screen 60.40.25 (prohibited signs) and 60.40.30 (size/height/location measurement rules).

**Wall sign essentials (60.40.35.1), for the common 3-wall-sign case:**
- Total wall-sign area = (primary wall length × wall height, height capped at 25 ft) × 20%. That product is
  the cumulative cap for ALL signs on the building; it may be reallocated across walls (60.40.35.1.B).
- Max area of any one wall sign = **120 sq ft**. Projection ≤ 16 inches from the wall.
- Multi-tenant buildings divide the 20% by lease frontage or via a master sign program.

**Completeness items typically needed for a sign permit:**
- Signed application form + owner/agent authorization (50.25.1.A).
- Site/plot plan showing each sign's location, setbacks from property lines and right-of-way, and vision-
  clearance triangles at driveways/intersections.
- Scaled sign drawings: dimensions and **area calculation per sign**, overall height, and projection.
- Building elevation(s) showing wall dimensions used in the 20% wall-area calculation and mounting height.
- For each wall sign, the wall it's mounted on and that wall's length/height (to verify the area allocation).
- Illumination details (type), material/colors where a downtown design standard applies.
- Cumulative tally of existing + proposed sign area on the site (60.40.30.1 counts existing signs).

**Known misinterpretations to avoid:**
- Do **not** require a structural/engineer's stamp for freestanding signs as a planning item — that is a
  Building Division routing matter, not a planning-completeness item.
- Signs **never** require Design Review. Do not flag a Design Review threshold for a sign.
- **Narrow exception only:** if a monument/freestanding sign is proposed *within a parking area* and its
  placement may intrude on vehicle maneuvering/parking area, note the *possible* parking/maneuvering
  implication for routing — this is a rare edge case, not a default item.

## Design Review Compliance Letter — DRCL, BDC 40.20.15.1 (Type 1)

DRCL is the **commercial / non-residential** Type 1 design path. Confirm the building is commercial,
industrial, or multiple-use (residential single-detached / middle housing goes to SDMHDR instead).

**Use DRCL only when the proposal is limited to the minor actions in 40.20.15.1.A, OR a floor-area addition
within these limits (40.20.15.1.2–.3):**
- Residential / commercial / multiple-use zones: GFA addition **≤ 25% of existing GFA AND ≤ 2,500 sq ft**.
- Industrial zones: GFA addition **≤ 15% of existing GFA AND < 30,000 sq ft**.

Above those limits → **Design Review Two** (Type 2) or **Three** (Type 3) — not this planner's Type 1 scope;
flag for routing/escalation. **There is no "500 sq ft" GFA threshold in the code — do not invent one.**

**Approval criteria (40.20.15.1.C):** threshold met; submittal complete (50.25.1); meets site development
standards of the applicable 20.xx.15 section; complies with Chapter 60 special requirements; grading per
60.15.10. Note: "visibility from the public right-of-way" is **not** a commercial Design Review factor (it is
only a Single-Detached DR consideration) — do not apply it to DRCL.

**Completeness items typically needed:** signed form + authorization; written statement addressing criteria;
existing + proposed GFA figures (to verify the threshold); site plan; elevations of the affected façade;
landscape/parking exhibits if those are part of the change.

## Single-Detached & Middle Housing Design Review One — SDMHDR1, BDC 40.21.15.1 (Type 1)

The **residential** Type 1 design path: single-detached dwellings and middle housing. Do **not** apply
commercial DR thresholds here.

**Thresholds (40.21.15.1.A)** include window/door additions or relocations subject to 60.05.60.2, removal of
Landscape Trees, and **floor-area additions for single-detached dwellings or middle housing in the RMA, RMB,
RMC, or CM-RM zones**, among others. Open 40.21.15.1 and read the current list against the proposal.

**Standards/guidelines for these housing types:** 60.05.60 (ch.60). Approval criteria at 40.21.15.1.C.

**Completeness items typically needed:** signed form + authorization; written statement; site plan; existing
and proposed elevations; floor-area figures; window/door schedule when those changes trigger the threshold.

## Legal Lot Determination — LLD, BDC 40.47 (Type 1)

Determines whether a parcel is a lawfully established lot. Open 40.47 for threshold, criteria, and submittal.
Typical completeness items: signed form + authorization; deeds/conveyance history establishing the lot;
recorded plat or survey references; tax-lot map.

## Landscape tree removal (note the path) — BDC 40.90.10.15, 40.20, 60.60

Per **40.90.10.15**, removal of **Landscape Trees** is covered by Design Review (40.20) and 60.60 (Trees and
Vegetation) — not the Tree Plan applications, which address Protected Trees, SNRAs, significant groves, etc.

**Known misinterpretations to avoid:**
- An **arborist letter is NOT required for landscape tree removal**. An arborist letter is for **hazard tree**
  removals — a hazard tree being one posing an imminent threat to people or structures (confirm the exact
  Chapter 90 definition live). A dead tree or a tree too close to a foundation, absent a hazard finding, is a
  landscape removal.
- **Fee-in-lieu is NOT a standard landscape-removal mitigation.** Fee-in-lieu applies to certain protected-tree
  removals. For landscape removals, identify the actual mitigation/replanting obligation under **60.60** and
  cite it; do not offer fee-in-lieu by default.
- Keep the **Resource Overlay / creek adjacency** screen — if the site abuts a creek or is within a mapped
  resource area, flag Resource Overlay (40.70 / 60.37) review.

## How to handle any other Chapter 40 application

For applications outside the four common ones, open the matching `40.xx.15 Application` section in
`chapter-40-applications.txt`. Read its **Threshold** (does the proposal qualify?), **Procedure Type**
(only Type 1 is in scope — if it's Type 2/3/4, say so and recommend routing), **Approval Criteria**
(these tell you which standards in chapters 20/60/70 the submittal must address), and **Submission
Requirements**. Build the checklist from the criteria + 50.25.1 + the standards those criteria cite.
